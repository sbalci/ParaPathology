# LFABP: Liver Fatty Acid Binding Protein

Diagnostic immunohistochemistry profile, subtyping algorithms, and diagnostic pitfalls for **Liver Fatty Acid Binding Protein (LFABP / FABP1)** based on [PathologyOutlines: LFABP](https://www.pathologyoutlines.com/topic/stainsLFABP.html) and international hepatopathology consensus guidelines.

---

## Executive Overview & Molecular Biology

| Feature | Specification |
|---|---|
| **Gene Symbol** | *FABP1* (HGNC: 3555; Entrez: 2168; Ensembl: ENSG00000163586) |
| **Chromosomal Locus** | Chromosome 2p11.2 |
| **Protein Name** | Liver-type Fatty Acid Binding Protein (L-FABP, FABP1, Z-protein) |
| **Molecular Mass** | ~14–15 kDa (127 amino acids) |
| **Primary Upstream Regulator** | **HNF1A** (Hepatocyte Nuclear Factor 1-alpha, chromosome 12q24.31) |
| **Normal Subcellular Localization** | Diffuse **cytoplasmic and nuclear** staining |
| **Physiological Function** | Intracellular carrier of long-chain fatty acids, eicosanoids, and bile acids; facilitates hepatic lipid transport, fatty acid β-oxidation, and protection against lipotoxicity |
| **Key Diagnostic Indication** | **Complete loss of expression** is the defining diagnostic hallmark of **HNF1A-inactivated hepatocellular adenoma (H-HCA)** |

---

## Normal Tissue Expression

LFABP is abundantly expressed in tissues with high fatty acid uptake and metabolism:
- **Liver:** Strong, diffuse, homogeneous cytoplasmic and nuclear positivity in **normal hepatocytes** throughout all zones (Zone 1 through Zone 3). *Biliary epithelial cells (cholangiocytes), Kupffer cells, and vascular endothelial cells are negative.*
- **Kidney:** Strong positivity in **renal proximal tubular epithelial cells** (pars convoluta and pars recta). Distal tubules, collecting ducts, and glomeruli are negative or trace.
- **Gastrointestinal Tract:** Moderate-to-strong cytoplasmic positivity in mucosal enterocytes of the **duodenum, jejunum, ileum, and cecum/colon** (decreasing along the craniocaudal axis). Gastric foveolar epithelium is normally negative (becomes positive in intestinal metaplasia).
- **Alveolar / Bronchial:** Negative.

```
┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│                        NORMAL TISSUE INTERNAL CONTROLS FOR LFABP                      │
└────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
  Normal Liver Parenchyma: 
    [ Hepatocyte Cytoplasm + Nucleus ]  ──► Strong Diffuse Positivity (3+)
    [ Bile Ducts / Kupffer Cells ]     ──► Internal Negative Control (0)
  
  Adjacent Renal Parenchyma:
    [ Proximal Convoluted Tubules ]     ──► Strong Positive Control (3+)
    [ Distal Tubules / Glomeruli ]      ──► Internal Negative Control (0)
```

## Diagnostic Applications in Surgical Pathology

### 1. Hepatocellular Adenoma (HCA) Subtyping (Bordeaux / WHO Classification)
Hepatocellular adenomas are benign liver neoplasms classified into molecular and clinicopathological subgroups. LFABP immunohistochemistry serves as an indispensable primary tier in the standard HCA diagnostic panel:

```
                                HEPATIC ADENOMA WORKUP
                                          │
                         Immunohistochemical Panel:
               [ LFABP, Serum Amyloid A (SAA) / CRP, Glutamine Synthetase (GS), β-Catenin ]
                                          │
        ┌─────────────────────────────────┼─────────────────────────────────┐
        ▼                                 ▼                                 ▼
   LFABP ABSENT                      SAA / CRP (+)                  β-Catenin Nuclear (+)
  (Complete Loss)                   (Strong Diffuse)                 & GS Diffuse Strong
        │                                 │                                 │
        ▼                                 ▼                                 ▼
    HNF1A-Inactivated              Inflammatory HCA                 β-Catenin-Mutated
      HCA (H-HCA)                      (I-HCA)                          HCA (B-HCA)
   • 30–40% of HCAs                • 40–50% of HCAs                 • 10–15% of HCAs
   • Diffuse steatosis             • Systemic inflammation          • High risk of HCC
   • Biallelic HNF1A mut           • IL6ST / STAT3 activation         transformation
   • Negligible HCC risk           • 10% co-mutate β-cat (B-IHCA)   • Exon 3 vs 7/8 mutations
```

#### Diagnostic Characteristics of H-HCA:
- **Pathognomonic Staining:** **Complete absence (loss of expression)** of LFABP in the neoplastic hepatocytes.
- **Mandatory Internal Control:** Non-neoplastic background liver parenchyma adjacent to or entrapped within the biopsy must demonstrate strong, diffuse cytoplasmic/nuclear LFABP positivity. If background liver is unstained, the run is analytically invalid.
- **Histomorphological Correlates:** Markedly diffuse microvesicular and macrovesicular steatosis (>80% of cases), lack of significant nuclear atypia, absence of pseudoglands or pseudoacini, and no thick trabeculae.
- **Clinical Significance:** H-HCA carries the lowest rate of malignant transformation (<1%) and minimal bleeding risk compared to other subtypes. Frequently observed in patients with maturity-onset diabetes of the young type 3 (MODY3) or familial adenomatosis.

### 2. Differential Diagnosis: Benign Liver Lesions vs. Hepatocellular Carcinoma

| Entity | LFABP | Glutamine Synthetase (GS) | SAA / CRP | Glypican-3 | CD34 Endothelial | Reticulin Framework |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Normal Liver** | **Positive (3+)** | Zone 3 perivenular rim (1–2 cell layers) | Negative | Negative | Periportal only | Intact (1–2 cell thick plates) |
| **Focal Nodular Hyperplasia (FNH)** | **Positive (3+)** | Characteristic **"map-like" / geographic anastomosing** | Negative | Negative | Preserved lobular | Intact, dystrophic large vessels, central scar |
| **HNF1A-Inactivated HCA (H-HCA)** | **ABSENT (0)** | Negative or faint non-zonal | Negative | Negative | Minimal patchy | Intact (isolated thin plates, no portal tracts) |
| **Inflammatory HCA (I-HCA)** | **Positive (3+)** | Negative or perivascular rim | **Diffuse Strong (+)** | Negative | Sinusoidal patchy | Intact, ductular reaction, dystrophic vessels |
| **β-Catenin Mutated HCA (B-HCA)** | **Positive (3+)** | **Strong diffuse homogeneous** | Negative (unless B-IHCA) | Negative | Patchy | Intact or focal loss |
| **Well-Differentiated HCC** | **Variable (Lost in ~20–30%)** | Often diffuse strong (if β-cat altered) | Variable | **Positive in ~60–70%** | **Diffuse diffuse capillarization** | **Fragmented or completely absent** |

## Critical Diagnostic Pitfalls

> [!CAUTION]
> **LFABP Loss Does NOT Equal Benignity:**
> Downregulation or complete loss of LFABP is observed in **15–30% of hepatocellular carcinomas (HCCs)**, particularly steatohepatitic HCC variants or HCCs arising from *HNF1A*-mutated backgrounds.
> 
> **Never diagnose an H-HCA based solely on LFABP loss if any features of malignancy are present:**
> 1. Reticulin framework loss (widened cords $\ge 3$ cells, fragmented reticulin meshwork).
> 2. Diffuse sinusoidal capillarization on CD34.
> 3. Nuclear atypia, prominent macronucleoli, or atypical mitotic figures.
> 4. Pseudoacinar/glandular architectural remodeling.
> 5. Diffuse Glypican-3 or HSP70 expression.

### Additional Pitfalls:
- **Patchy or Heterogeneous Staining:** True H-HCA exhibits complete, total loss of LFABP throughout the entire nodule. Heterogeneous, patchy, or mosaic staining should raise suspicion for an alternative HCA subtype, focal clonal escape, or a well-differentiated HCC.
- **Biopsy Sampling Limitation:** Needle biopsies lacking adjacent non-neoplastic liver parenchyma deprive the pathologist of the mandatory internal positive control. If background hepatocytes are absent and the lesion is negative, report as *indeterminate for LFABP evaluation* unless repeat cutting captures non-lesional liver.

## Secondary Diagnostic Uses

### 1. Renal Cell Neoplasm Subtyping
Because LFABP is normally expressed in proximal tubules:
- **Clear Cell Renal Cell Carcinoma (ccRCC):** Typically **positive** (cytoplasmic and membranous/nuclear, ~70–85% of cases).
- **Papillary Renal Cell Carcinoma (pRCC):** Frequently **positive** (~60–80%).
- **Chromophobe Renal Cell Carcinoma (chRCC):** Typically **negative** or only weak, focal positivity.
- **Renal Oncocytoma:** Typically **negative**.
- *Clinical application:* Useful adjunct in challenging oncocytic/eosinophilic renal neoplasms when differentiating eosinophilic ccRCC from chRCC or oncocytoma.

### 2. Gastrointestinal Adenocarcinomas
- Serves as a phenotypic marker of enterocytic differentiation.
- Positive in small intestinal adenocarcinomas and intestinal-type gastric adenocarcinomas; negative in diffuse/signet-ring gastric carcinomas and non-intestinal foregut neoplasms.

## Laboratory & Technical Specifications

| Parameter | Recommended Specification |
|---|---|
| **Representative Clones** | **C-4** (mouse monoclonal, Santa Cruz); **EP224** (rabbit monoclonal); **EPR2189** (rabbit monoclonal, Abcam) |
| **Antigen Retrieval (HIER)** | Heat-Induced Epitope Retrieval in **High pH buffer (Tris-EDTA, pH 9.0)** at 95–99°C for 20–30 min |
| **Recommended Detection System** | 2-step or 3-step polymer-based horseradish peroxidase (HRP) with DAB chromogen |
| **Positive Control Tissue** | Normal liver parenchyma (hepatocytes 3+) or normal kidney cortex (proximal tubules 3+) |
| **Negative Control Tissue** | Tonsil, lymphoid tissue, or omission of primary antibody |
| **Quality Reporting Standard** | Report as: (1) Intact/Retained expression, (2) Complete loss of expression (specifying presence of positive internal control), or (3) Equivocal/Suboptimal internal control |
