## Summary

A comprehensive state-of-the-art review by **Anandi Lobo** (Center for Pathology and Urology, Raipur) and **Liang Cheng** (Warren Alpert Medical School of Brown University & Lifespan Academic Medical Center) published in *Endocrine Pathology* (DOI: [10.1007/s12022-026-09932-0](https://doi.org/10.1007/s12022-026-09932-0)).

The review integrates histopathological, immunophenotypic, and genomic findings to address the fundamental shortcomings of the current **2022 WHO classification** of urinary bladder neuroendocrine neoplasms (NENs). Because the WHO framework was directly extrapolated from gastroenteropancreatic and pulmonary models, it fails to account for unique bladder-specific biology — notably the overwhelming predominance of high-grade neuroendocrine carcinomas (NECs), their common clonal origin with conventional urothelial carcinoma (UC), and the practical absence of well-differentiated high-grade tumors (NET G3). 

Lobo & Cheng formulate an organ-specific, refined 4-category classification system combining mitotic rate, Ki-67 proliferation index, morphology, and molecular drivers, while outlining emerging transcriptomic lineage subgroups (ASCL1, NEUROD1, POU2F3, triple-negative) and therapeutic horizons (DLL3-targeted bispecific T-cell engagers like tarlatamab).

## Why It Matters for Clinical & Surgical Pathology

1. **Mandatory Reporting of Any Neuroendocrine Component:**
   In bladder cancer, finding *even minor focal* small cell or large cell neuroendocrine carcinoma (<10%) radically alters clinical care. Standard transurethral resection (TURBT) or bladder-sparing gemcitabine-cisplatin regimens are inadequate; patients require systemic **neoadjuvant chemotherapy (NAC) with platinum + etoposide (EP)** prior to radical cystectomy due to extreme propensity for early micrometastatic dissemination.
2. **Resolution of Diagnostic Traps:**
   - **TTF-1 (NKX2-1) is positive in 30–40% of primary bladder SCNECs!** Pathologists who interpret TTF-1 positivity as proof of metastasis from small-cell lung cancer (SCLC) commit a critical misdiagnosis.
   - **GATA3 is positive in 30–50% of bladder paragangliomas**, which can lead to catastrophic confusion with muscle-invasive urothelial carcinoma if cytokeratins and neuroendocrine/sustentacular markers are omitted.
3. **Anatomic Tie-Breaker via TERT Promoter Sequencing:**
   When patients present with synchronous lung and bladder tumors, or concurrent prostatic and bladder masses, *TERT* promoter mutation testing (*C228T* / *C250T*) serves as a definitive bladder lineage discriminator: present in **70–80%** of bladder SCNECs, but virtually absent in pulmonary SCLC and rare in prostatic small cell carcinoma.

## Pathogenesis: Divergent Urothelial Lineage vs. Resident Cells

Historically debated whether bladder NENs arise from resident Kultschitzky-type neuroendocrine cells, metaplastic epithelium, or stem cells. Contemporary molecular biology strongly establishes **divergent differentiation from transformed pluripotent urothelial stem cells**:

- **Frequent Co-existence:** Over 50% of bladder SCNECs and LCNECs present with concurrent non-neuroendocrine components (conventional invasive UC, high-grade urothelial carcinoma in situ [CIS], squamous cell carcinoma, or adenocarcinoma).
- **Shared Clonal Fingerprint:** Laser-capture microdissection studies demonstrate that both components share identical loss-of-heterozygosity (LOH) patterns, identical *TP53* mutations, identical *RB1* deletions, and identical *TERT* promoter mutations.
- **Contrast with Prostatic Neuroendocrine Carcinoma:** In prostate cancer, neuroendocrine carcinoma predominantly emerges as a secondary, treatment-induced lineage plasticity switch under androgen receptor pathway inhibition (ARSI). In the bladder, SCNEC/LCNEC develops *de novo* or concurrently as part of primary clonal evolution, with no established treatment-emergent transdifferentiation paradigm following standard cytotoxic chemotherapy or immunotherapy.

### The Critical Omission: Why "NET G3" Does Not Belong in the Bladder
In the pancreas and digestive tract, NET G3 represents a well-differentiated tumor with organoid morphology, intact *TP53*/*RB1*, but elevated Ki-67 (>20%). In the urinary bladder, rigorous review of published literature and institutional series identifies **no reproducible cases of true NET G3**. Every bladder tumor exhibiting Ki-67 >20% and high mitotic activity shows the cytologic features, mutational profile (*TP53* and *RB1* co-inactivation), and aggressive clinical course of a poorly differentiated NEC (SCNEC or LCNEC). Retaining a NET G3 category in the bladder risks undertreating aggressive NECs with indolent somatostatin analogue protocols.

## Four Lineage Transcription Factor Subtypes in Bladder NECs

Paralleling the molecular classification of pulmonary small-cell carcinoma (SCLC-A, SCLC-N, SCLC-P, SCLC-I), bladder SCNEC and LCNEC harbor four distinct lineage subsets:

1. **ASCL1-Dominant (SCLC-A):** Classic high-neuroendocrine phenotype with robust neuroendocrine granules, high Synaptophysin/Chromogranin expression, and elevated **DLL3** levels.
2. **NEUROD1-Dominant (SCLC-N):** Intermediate neuroendocrine profile, frequent neuronal differentiation programs.
3. **POU2F3-Dominant (SCLC-P):** Tuft cell-like differentiation. Tumors are often **negative or weak for conventional neuroendocrine markers** (Synaptophysin/Chromogranin negative, but positive for POU2F3 and GFI1B). **Demonstrates significantly shorter progression-free survival (PFS) and overall survival (OS)**, representing an ultra-aggressive variant.
4. **Triple-Negative / Inflamed (SCLC-I):** Low expression of all three master transcription factors. Characterized by mesenchymal transitions, HLA upregulation, and dense immune infiltration — representing the primary candidate subset for immune checkpoint blockade.

| Differential Diagnosis | Recommended IHC Panel | Diagnostic Clues & Pitfalls |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Primary Bladder SCNEC vs. Metastatic Lung SCLC** | INSM1, Synaptophysin, Chromogranin, TTF-1, GATA3, CK903, *TERT* promoter NGS | **TTF-1 is positive in 30–40% of bladder SCNECs** (cannot rule out bladder primary). *TERT* promoter mutation (*C228T/C250T*) is positive in ~80% of bladder SCNECs, negative in lung SCLC. |
| **Bladder SCNEC vs. Prostatic Small Cell Carcinoma** | PSA, PSMA, NKX3.1, AR, ERG, GATA3, *TERT* promoter NGS | Prostatic small cell is frequently negative for PSA/PSMA/NKX3.1 due to dedifferentiation. *TERT* promoter mutation confirms bladder urothelial lineage; prostate SCNEC lacks *TERT* mutations. |
| **Bladder SCNEC vs. High-Grade Urothelial Carcinoma** | INSM1, Synaptophysin, GATA3, p63, RB1 | High-grade UC can focally express neuroendocrine markers. **INSM1** provides crisp nuclear specificity. Complete loss of RB1 strongly supports SCNEC. |

## Therapeutic Horizons & The Immunotherapy Paradox

- **Neoadjuvant Platinum-Etoposide (EP):** Standard of care for muscle-invasive or high-risk bladder SCNEC prior to radical cystectomy.
- **The Immunotherapy Paradox:** Bladder SCNEC exhibits high tumor mutational burden (TMB) driven by APOBEC cytidine deaminases, yet immune checkpoint inhibitor (ICI) monotherapy has shown disappointing clinical activity. Histopathologically, the tumor microenvironment is an **"immune desert" or "immune excluded"** niche with low PD-L1 expression (0–50%), dense peritumoral extracellular matrix, and scarce intratumoral CD8+ T-cell infiltration.
- **Ineffectiveness of Bladder UC ADCs:** Enfortumab vedotin (targeting Nectin-4) and Sacituzumab govitecan (targeting TROP2) have low or absent target expression in pure SCNEC.
- **DLL3-Targeted Precision Therapy (Tarlatamab):** Downregulation of Notch signaling causes high cell-surface expression of Delta-like ligand 3 (DLL3). Following the landmark success of **tarlatamab** (bispecific T-cell engager / BiTE targeting DLL3 and CD3; demonstrated dramatic overall survival improvements in SCLC in DeLLphi-301 and Phase 3 DeLLphi-304), DLL3 represents the most promising biomarker-driven target for bladder SCNEC.
